# Chapter 1 – Gair Marusi Tenants

## Definition

A Gair Marusi is generally regarded as a non-occupancy tenant or tenant-at-will.

The expression alone does not establish:

* Ownership
* Occupancy rights
* Adverse possession

Every case must be examined on its facts.

---

## Core Litigation Questions

### Question 1

Did tenancy actually exist?

Examine:

* Rent entries
* Batai entries
* Chakota entries
* Lease records

---

### Question 2

Was possession permissive?

If possession originated from landlord consent:

* Ownership claims become difficult.

---

### Question 3

Can long possession convert into ownership?

Generally:

No.

Long possession alone is insufficient.

Courts require proof of:

* Statutory protection
* Occupancy rights
* Adverse possession

---

# Chapter 2 – Occupancy Rights

## Nature

Occupancy rights are stronger than ordinary tenancy rights.

Characteristics:

* Heritable
* Transferable in limited circumstances
* Protected from arbitrary ejectment

---

## Evidence Required

Strong evidence includes:

* Historical jamabandis
* Settlement records
* Wajib-ul-Arz
* Misal Haqiat

---

## Weak Evidence

* Recent girdawari entries
* Oral testimony
* Uncorroborated possession claims

---

# Chapter 3 – Punjab Occupancy Tenants (Vesting of Proprietary Rights) Act, 1952

## Importance

One of the most overlooked statutes.

Many occupancy tenants became owners under this legislation.

---

## Litigation Checklist

Determine:

1. Was claimant occupancy tenant?
2. Was land covered by vesting legislation?
3. Was proprietary rights mutation sanctioned?

---

## Critical Documents

* Pre-vesting jamabandi
* Mutation register
* Vesting records
* Proprietary rights mutation

## Meaning

These expressions usually indicate self-cultivation by the proprietor.

---

## Legal Significance

* Khudkasht

often defeat later tenancy claims.

---

## Important Sources

* Settlement records

# Chapter 5 – Adverse Possession Against Landlord

## Essential Elements

Claimant must establish:

1. Hostile possession
2. Open denial of title
3. Knowledge of landlord
4. Continuous possession
5. Statutory limitation period

---

## Common Failure

Many claimants prove possession.

Few prove hostility.

Possession alone is not adverse possession.

---

# Chapter 6 – Adverse Possession Among Co-Sharers

## Extremely Difficult Claim

Courts consistently require:

* Ouster
* Exclusive hostile possession
* Notice to co-sharers

---

## Presumption

Possession of one co-sharer remains possession of all co-sharers.

---

# Chapter 7 – Co-Sharer Possession

## Foundational Rule

Every co-sharer owns every inch of joint property.

---

## Consequences

One co-sharer may:

* Cultivate land
* Enjoy produce
* Possess specific parcels

without becoming exclusive owner.

---

## Key Cases

### Bhartu v. Ram Sarup

Leading Full Bench authority.

### Sant Ram Nagina Ram v. Daya Ram Nagina Ram

Foundational co-sharer judgment.

---

# Chapter 8 – Sale by Co-Sharer

## Common Situation

Vendor sells:

* Specific rectangle
* Specific killa
* Specific khasra number

from a joint holding.

---

## Legal Position

Purchaser generally acquires:

* Vendor's share
* Vendor's possessory rights

subject to future partition.

---

# Chapter 9 – Injunction Between Co-Sharers

## General Rule

One co-sharer ordinarily cannot restrain another co-sharer merely because he is using joint land.

---

## Exceptions

Injunction may be granted where:

* Waste is occurring
* Permanent injury is threatened
* Ouster is attempted

---

# Chapter 10 – Partition Proceedings

## Purpose

Partition converts:

Joint possession

into

Separate possession.

---

## Authorities Involved

### Assistant Collector

Initial partition proceedings.

### Collector

Appeals.

### Commissioner

Further appeals.

### Financial Commissioner

Revisionary jurisdiction.

---

# Chapter 11 – Mode of Partition

## Important Considerations

Revenue authorities generally consider:

* Existing possession
* Quality of land
* Irrigation
* Access
* Equity among co-sharers

---

## Practical Rule

The object is fair partition rather than mathematical perfection.

---

# Chapter 12 – Revenue vs Civil Court Jurisdiction

## Revenue Authorities

Generally decide:

* Partition
* Mutation
* Certain tenancy matters

---

## Civil Courts

Generally decide:

* Ownership disputes
* Fraud
* Declaration of title
* Cancellation of documents

---

## Litigation Strategy

Always identify jurisdiction before filing proceedings.

---

# Chapter 13 – Mutation Proceedings

## Nature

Mutation proceedings are administrative.

---

## Important Principle

Mutation:

* Does not create ownership
* Does not extinguish ownership

---

## Practical Use

Mutation assists:

* Revenue collection
* Record maintenance

---

# Chapter 14 – Khasra Girdawari

## Purpose

Records:

* Cultivator
* Crop
* Possession

---

## Evidentiary Value

Useful evidence.

Not conclusive proof of title.

# Chapter 15 – Jamabandi
