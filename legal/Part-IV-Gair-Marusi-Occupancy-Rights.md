# Part IV – Gair Marusi & Occupancy Rights

## Practitioner's Handbook for Punjab & Haryana

---

# Introduction

This handbook focuses exclusively on:

* Gair Marusi tenants
* Occupancy tenants
* Khudkasht / Khanakasht
* Ejectment proceedings
* Proprietary rights
* Historical tenancy records
* Revenue evidence

Many disputes involving agricultural land ultimately depend upon the correct interpretation of tenancy entries in revenue records.

---

# Chapter 1 – Understanding Gair Marusi

## Meaning

The expression "Gair Marusi" generally refers to a non-occupancy tenant.

Historically it indicates possession under another person's title without the higher status of an occupancy tenant.

---

## What Gair Marusi Does NOT Mean

A Gair Marusi entry does not automatically establish:

* Ownership
* Occupancy rights
* Adverse possession
* Proprietary title

Every case requires examination of supporting records.

---

## Practical Rule

Always ask:

1. How did possession begin?
2. Was rent paid?
3. Was possession permissive?
4. Is there evidence of occupancy rights?

---

# Chapter 2 – Types of Agricultural Possession

## Proprietor

Owner recorded in ownership column.

---

## Occupancy Tenant

Protected tenant possessing statutory rights.

---

## Non-Occupancy Tenant

Ordinary tenant with comparatively weaker protection.

---

## Gair Marusi

Generally treated as a non-occupancy tenant unless evidence establishes otherwise.

---

## Mortgagee in Possession

Possession derived from mortgage transaction.

---

## Licensee

Possession without tenancy rights.

---

# Chapter 3 – Occupancy Rights

## Importance

Occupancy rights represent one of the strongest forms of tenancy recognized under the Punjab Tenancy Act.

---

## Characteristics

Occupancy rights are generally:

* Heritable
* Transferable in limited circumstances
* Protected against ejectment

---

## Sources of Occupancy Rights

Occupancy rights may arise through:

* Statute
* Historical recognition
* Revenue records
* Settlement proceedings

---

### Misal Haqiat

## Supporting Evidence

* Rent entries

# Chapter 5 – Punjab Tenancy Act, 1887

## Central Statute

Most tenancy disputes derive from this Act.

---

## Major Areas

### Occupancy Rights

Sections dealing with recognition of protected tenancy.

---

### Ejectment

Grounds upon which tenants may be removed.

---

### Rent

Evidence of tenancy relationship.

---

### Succession

Heritable nature of certain tenancy rights.

---

# Chapter 6 – Punjab Occupancy Tenants (Vesting of Proprietary Rights) Act, 1952

## Importance

A transformative statute.

Many occupancy tenants became proprietors under its provisions.

---

## Key Litigation Question

Was the claimant an occupancy tenant on the relevant date?

If yes:

The dispute may become one concerning ownership rather than tenancy.

---

## Important Documents

* Pre-vesting Jamabandi
* Occupancy entries
* Mutation orders
* Proprietary rights mutations

---

# Chapter 7 – Khudkasht

## Meaning

Land cultivated by the proprietor himself.

---

## Legal Significance

Repeated Khudkasht entries often weaken claims of protected tenancy.

---

## Documents to Examine

* Settlement records
* Jamabandis
* Girdawaris
* Misal Haqiat

---

# Chapter 8 – Khanakasht

## Meaning

Historically associated with self-cultivation by proprietor.

---

## Practical Impact

If old records consistently show Khanakasht:

Subsequent claims of occupancy tenancy may face serious difficulty.

---

# Chapter 9 – Makbuja Malkan

## Meaning

Possession of proprietors.

---

## Litigation Importance

Often relied upon by landowners to rebut tenancy claims.

---

## Factors Considered

# Chapter 12 – Long Possession

## Common Misconception

Many litigants believe:

Long possession = ownership.

This is incorrect.

---

## Court's Inquiry

Courts ask:

1. Was possession permissive?
2. Was landlord's title acknowledged?
3. Was rent paid?
4. Was possession hostile?

---

# Chapter 13 – Adverse Possession by Tenant

## Difficult Claim

A tenant generally cannot claim adverse possession while continuing to recognize landlord's title.

---

## Requirements

* Open hostility
* Denial of title
* Knowledge of landlord
* Continuous possession

---

# Chapter 15 – Common Litigation Scenarios

## Scenario A

Gair Marusi for 60 years.

Questions:

* Was rent paid?
* Was occupancy status recognized?
* Was proprietary title ever acquired?

---

## Scenario B

Khudkasht entry in old records.

Question:

Can tenancy still be established?

Requires strong contrary evidence.

---

## Scenario C

Occupancy rights claimed after consolidation.

Review:

* Pre-consolidation records
* Vesting legislation
* Mutation history

# Chapter 17 – Important Legal Propositions

## Proposition 1

Gair Marusi does not automatically become owner.

---

## Proposition 2

Occupancy rights require proof.

---

## Proposition 3

Mutation does not create title.

---

## Proposition 4

Historical records often outweigh current entries.

---

## Proposition 5

Long possession alone is insufficient.

---

## Proposition 6

Khudkasht entries can defeat tenancy claims.

---

## Proposition 7

Rent entries are frequently decisive.

---

# Practitioner Notes

Always start research from:

1. Settlement records.
2. Misal Haqiat.
3. Wajib-ul-Arz.

Only afterwards examine:

4. Jamabandi.
5. Girdawari.
6. Mutation.

Many tenancy disputes are won by one overlooked historical entry.
