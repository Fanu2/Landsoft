# SECTION A – CO-SHARER RIGHTS

---

## A-1. Bhartu v. Ram Sarup

### Full Bench

### Issue

Rights of co-sharers in joint property.

### Principle

Every co-sharer is owner of every inch of joint property until lawful partition.

### Key Holdings

* Possession of one co-sharer is possession of all.
* Exclusive cultivation is not exclusive ownership.
* Sale of specific khasra numbers remains subject to partition.
* Ouster is not presumed.

### Practical Use

Use in:

* Partition disputes
* Injunction cases
* Specific khasra sales
* Adverse possession claims among co-sharers

### Keywords

Bhartu, Co-sharer, Ouster, Joint Possession

---

## A-2. Sant Ram Nagina Ram v. Daya Ram Nagina Ram

### Issue

Possession among co-sharers.

### Principle

Long possession does not automatically destroy rights of other co-sharers.

### Key Holdings

* Ouster must be proved.
* Joint ownership continues unless lawfully terminated.
* Mere cultivation is insufficient.

### Practical Use

Used in:

* Adverse possession disputes
* Co-sharer possession litigation
* Declaratory suits

### Keywords

Sant Ram, Co-owner, Joint Possession

---

## A-3. Jai Singh v. Gurmej Singh

### Issue

Sale by co-sharer.

### Principle

Purchaser acquires rights of vendor co-sharer.

### Key Holdings

* Purchase remains subject to partition.
* Purchaser steps into shoes of vendor.

### Keywords

Sale, Co-sharer, Partition

# SECTION B – PARTITION LAW

---

## B-1. Fundamental Principle

Partition converts:

Joint enjoyment

into

Separate enjoyment.

---

## B-2. Revenue Officer Jurisdiction

Revenue authorities may decide:

* Shares
* Mode of partition
* Allocation

Revenue authorities ordinarily do not decide:

* Ownership declarations
* Fraud allegations
* Complex title disputes

---

## B-3. Sanad Taksim

### Importance

Final partition instrument.

### Effect

Creates separate holdings.

### Litigation Value

One of the strongest documents in partition disputes.

---

# SECTION C – INJUNCTIONS BETWEEN CO-SHARERS

---

## C-1. General Rule

A co-sharer ordinarily cannot restrain another co-sharer from using joint land.

---

## C-2. Exceptions

Injunction may be granted where:

* Waste is committed.
* Permanent injury is threatened.
* Ouster is attempted.

---

## C-3. Practical Rule

Where dispute concerns possession alone:

Partition is usually the proper remedy.

---

# SECTION D – GAIR MARUSI TENANTS

---

## D-1. Fundamental Proposition

Gair Marusi does not automatically mean:

* Owner
* Occupancy Tenant
* Adverse Possessor

## D-2. Litigation Questions

# SECTION E – OCCUPANCY RIGHTS

---

## E-1. Core Principle

Occupancy rights arise through statute.

They are not presumed merely from possession.

---

## E-2. Important Evidence

* Historical tenancy records
* Settlement records
* Wajib-ul-Arz
* Misal Haqiat

---

## E-3. Typical Litigation Issues

* Hereditary rights
* Ejectment protection
* Proprietary rights under vesting legislation

# SECTION F – KHUDKASHT AND KHANAKASHT

---

## F-1. Meaning

Generally indicates self-cultivation by proprietor.

---

## F-2. Legal Effect

Historical entries showing:

* Khudkasht

* Makbuja Malkan

often weaken later tenancy claims.

---

## F-3. Best Evidence

1. Settlement records

3. Wajib-ul-Arz

# SECTION G – ADVERSE POSSESSION

---

## G-1. Against Landlord

Must prove:

* Hostility
* Open denial
* Knowledge
* Continuity

Possession alone is insufficient.

---

## G-2. Among Co-Sharers

Extremely difficult.

Requirements:

* Ouster
* Exclusive hostile possession
* Notice to co-sharers

---

# SECTION H – SALE OF SPECIFIC KHASRA NUMBERS

---

## H-1. Common Situation

Vendor sells:

* Killa
* Rectangle
* Specific field

from joint holding.

---

## H-2. Legal Effect

Purchaser generally acquires:

* Vendor's share
* Vendor's possessory rights

subject to partition.

---

# SECTION I – MUTATION LAW

---

## I-1. Fundamental Principle

Mutation does not:

* Create title
* Transfer title
* Extinguish title

---

## I-2. Purpose

Revenue administration.

---

## I-3. Litigation Use

Mutation is evidence.

It is not conclusive proof of ownership.

---

# SECTION J – JAMABANDI

---

## J-1. Presumption

Entries carry presumption of correctness.

---

## J-2. Limitation

Presumption is rebuttable.

---

## J-3. Litigation Use

Useful starting point.

Never rely on Jamabandi alone.
