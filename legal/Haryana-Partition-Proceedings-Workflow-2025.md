# Haryana Partition Proceedings Workflow (2025 Edition)

## Practical Guide to Partition of Khewat

---

# Introduction

Partition proceedings are the principal legal mechanism for separating joint agricultural holdings in Haryana.

Most rural disputes involving:

* Joint Khewats
* Co-sharers
* Agricultural land
* Possession disputes
* Sale of undivided shares

ultimately require partition proceedings before revenue authorities.

---

# Statutory Basis

Partition proceedings are governed primarily by:

## Punjab Land Revenue Act, 1887

Applicable in Haryana with amendments.

---

# Objective of Partition

Convert:

Joint Ownership

and

Joint Possession

into

Separate Ownership

and

Separate Possession

# Step 2 – Preliminary Scrutiny

Revenue Officer examines:

* Whether Khewat is joint.
* Whether applicant has share.
* Whether land is partitionable.

---

# Step 3 – Notice to Co-Sharers

All recorded co-sharers are served.

---

## Common Objections

### Wrong Share

### Applicant not Co-sharer

### Civil Litigation Pending

### Previous Partition Exists

---

# Step 4 – Determination of Shares

Revenue Officer verifies:

* Ownership entries
* Mutations
* Court decrees
* Inheritance records

---

# Practical Rule

Partition cannot proceed correctly until shares are determined.

---

# Step 5 – Preparation of Naksha Alif

## Purpose

Map of entire joint holding.

Shows:

* Khasra numbers
* Area
* Possession
* Roads
* Watercourses

---

# Litigation Importance

Errors at this stage create future disputes.

---

# Step 6 – Mode of Partition (Naksha Bey)

One of the most important stages.

---

## Factors Considered

### Existing Possession

### Quality of Land

### Irrigation

### Access

### Location

### Equity

---

## Objective

To partition fairly while disturbing possession as little as possible.

---

# Step 7 – Objections to Mode of Partition

Common objections:

---

## No Road Access

Landlocked allotment.

---

## Unequal Land Quality

Inferior soil allotted.

---

## Irrigation Inequality

Canal access affected.

---

## Existing Possession Ignored

Long cultivation disturbed unnecessarily.

---

# Step 8 – Naksha Kha

Prepared after mode of partition is approved.

Shows proposed allotment to each shareholder.

---

# Objections to Naksha Kha

Typical objections:

### Wrong Area

### Wrong Boundaries

### Access Problems

### Irrigation Problems

---

# Step 9 – Final Partition Map

After objections decided.

Revenue Officer finalizes:

* Allotments
* Boundaries
* Shares

---

# Step 10 – Sanad Taksim

## Most Important Document

Instrument of partition.

---

## Legal Effect

Joint ownership ends.

Separate holdings arise.

---

## Practical Importance

Equivalent to final partition order.

---

# Step 11 – Delivery of Possession

Where necessary:

Revenue authorities deliver possession according to Sanad Taksim.

---

# Existing Possession Principle

Revenue authorities generally try:

* To preserve possession.
* To avoid unnecessary disturbance.

---

## Important Limitation

Existing possession does not override ownership shares.

---

# Purchaser of Specific Khasra Number

Common Haryana issue.

---

## Example

Co-sharer sells:

Khasra No. 25//8

from joint khewat.

---

## Legal Position

Purchaser generally acquires:

* Vendor's share
* Vendor's rights

subject to partition.

# Haryana-Specific Practical Advice

Always inspect:

* Road access
* Canal access
* Watercourse rights
* Existing tube wells
* Existing possession
* Consolidation history

Many partition disputes are won or lost on these practical issues rather than abstract legal principles.
