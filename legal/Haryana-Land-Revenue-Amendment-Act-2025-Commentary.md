# Haryana Land Revenue Amendment Act, 2025

## Practical Commentary for Revenue Officers, Lawyers and Landowners

---

# Introduction

The Haryana Land Revenue (Amendment) Act, 2025 is one of the most significant changes in Haryana revenue law in recent decades.

The amendment primarily targets:

* Long-pending joint khewats
* Undivided agricultural holdings
* Co-sharer disputes
* Delayed partition proceedings

The legislative intent appears to be:

> Reduce litigation and encourage separation of long-standing joint holdings.

---

# Historical Background

## Traditional Position

Before 2025:

Partition usually commenced only when:

* A co-sharer applied.
* A purchaser sought separation.
* A legal heir demanded partition.

Many khewats remained joint for decades.

### Endless Co-sharer Disputes

# Chapter 2 – Amended Section 111A

## New Heading

Partition in case of joint holdings between land owners except where co-sharers are husband and wife.

---

# Practical Effect

Revenue authorities now possess broader powers to initiate partition proceedings.

---

# Chapter 3 – Suo Motu Partition

## Meaning

Revenue Officer may initiate proceedings even without application by a co-sharer.

---

# Why Introduced?

Government identified large numbers of:

* Unpartitioned khewats
* Long-standing disputes
* Multiple competing possession claims

---

# Practical Consequences

Revenue officials can issue notices requiring co-sharers to address partition.

# Chapter 6 – Husband and Wife Exception

## Special Provision

Automatic partition provisions generally do not apply where co-sharers are husband and wife.

---

# Practical Purpose

To avoid unnecessary fragmentation of family holdings.
