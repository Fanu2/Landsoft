# Partition of Khewat

## Punjab & Haryana Revenue Law Handbook

---

# Introduction

Partition of Khewat is one of the most frequently litigated subjects in Punjab and Haryana.

Most disputes arise due to:

* Joint ownership
* Sale of undivided shares
* Long possession by one co-sharer
* Agricultural cultivation
* Access disputes
* Canal irrigation disputes
* Boundary disputes

The primary objective of partition is:

> To convert joint ownership and joint possession into separate ownership and separate possession.

---

# Chapter 1 – What is a Khewat?

A Khewat is an ownership account maintained in the revenue record.

It shows:

* Owners
* Their shares
* Revenue liability

---

## Example

Khewat No. 125

| Owner | Share |
| ----- | ----- |
| A     | 1/2   |
| B     | 1/4   |
| C     | 1/4   |

All three owners jointly own the entire khewat until partition.

---

# Chapter 2 – Difference Between Khewat, Khatoni and Khasra

## Khewat

Ownership account.

---

## Khatoni

Cultivation account.

---

## Khasra

Individual field number.

---

## Practical Example

One Khewat may contain:

* Several Khatonis
* Dozens of Khasra numbers

# Chapter 7 – Co-Sharer Rights

## Fundamental Principle

Every co-sharer owns every inch of joint property.

---

## Consequences

Possession of one co-sharer is presumed possession of all co-sharers.

---

## Important Principle

Exclusive cultivation does not automatically create exclusive ownership.

---

# Chapter 8 – Existing Possession

Revenue authorities usually attempt to preserve existing possession where possible.

---

## Why?

To minimize disputes.

To avoid disturbing long-standing cultivation.

To ensure equitable partition.

# Chapter 10 – Sale of Specific Khasra Numbers

A common dispute in Haryana and Punjab.

---

## Example

A owns 1/2 share in joint khewat.

A sells Khasra No. 25/8.

---

## Legal Position

Purchaser generally acquires:

* Vendor's share
* Possessory rights

subject to final partition.

# Chapter 11 – Long Possession

## Common Argument

"I have cultivated this land for 40 years."

---

## Legal Position

Long possession alone does not defeat rights of co-sharers.

---

## Ouster Must Be Proved

Requirements:

* Hostile possession
* Knowledge of co-sharers
* Continuous exclusion

# Chapter 21 – Practical Examples

## Example 1

Khewat = 16 Kanal

A = 1/2

B = 1/4

C = 1/4

Partition Result:

A = 8 Kanal

B = 4 Kanal

C = 4 Kanal

subject to quality and possession adjustments.

---

## Example 2

Purchaser buys 2 Kanal from co-sharer.

Purchaser receives rights of vendor.

Specific parcel remains subject to partition.

---

## Example 3

Co-sharer cultivates same land for 30 years.

Result:

Still subject to rights of other co-sharers unless ouster proved.

# Common Mistakes

1. Assuming possession equals ownership.
2. Ignoring old revenue records.
3. Ignoring consolidation records.
4. Relying only on mutation entries.
5. Ignoring access and irrigation issues.
6. Not challenging Naksha Bey in time.
